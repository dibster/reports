01 / Read this first

The shortlist, with the catches left in.

Best place to investigate first

Playa San Juan, Tenerife

A functioning harbour town, sheltered beach, promenade and independent restaurants. It has the strongest combination of everyday town life and concrete house leads in this research.

The catches: the house-and-garden brief is not yet secured at a suitable long-stay rate, dogs cannot simply use the bathing beach, and a large dog may exceed mainland ferry cabin limits.

Explore Playa San Juan →

Closest alternative

Alcalá, Tenerife

The appealing combination here is the town centre, coastal promenade, volcanic coves and natural pools. A good candidate for walking to the sea each morning and eating locally.

The catches: a truly walkable private house with a garden remains unverified. There is resort development beside the town, and the same beach-access and ferry questions apply.

Explore Alcalá →

Warmth is achievable; the complete Cap Ferret package is not established. Southern Canary station averages are close to 21–22°C, with substantially milder nights than Agadir. But none of the five towns has a verified combination of suitable house, garden, dog arrangements and transport. These are ranked research leads, not ready-to-book recommendations.

02 / The geography

Farther south changes the weather.

  • 1 & 2 · Southwest Tenerife Alcalá and Playa San Juan. The leading climate-and-town-life candidates to investigate.
  • 3 · South La Gomera Playa Santiago. A quieter alternative, with a smaller dining scene and more challenging terrain.
  • 4 · Southwest Gran Canaria Arguineguín. Local harbour life alongside Nordic-oriented services and tourism.

“20–22°C most days” is a tougher test than a 22°C average high. A maximum is often reached only briefly in the afternoon. Shade, cloud and wind can feel much cooler. 04 / A place to live, not just visit

Five towns. Five different compromises.

Atmosphere judgments describe the visible character and tourism offer. There are no fabricated nationality percentages and no guarantee of “no Brits”.

01

Guía de Isora · Southwest Tenerife

Alcalá

Promenade & covesHousing unproven

The one to look at if a small-town centre and a long seaside stroll matter more than a broad golden beach.

The scene / British influence

Our judgment: a better prospect for understated coastal living than a resort corridor. The town has its own centre and waterfront, but a major hotel resort sits alongside it. This is not a secluded villa peninsula, and there is no verified evidence of a negligible British presence.

Daytime warmth

21.7°C January / 22.0°C February at Tenerife South Airport. Use these as a regional guide, not an Alcalá-specific guarantee. Choose a low coastal location and check the actual terrace’s sun and exposure.

Coast & everyday walking

The official island guide confirms the Alcalá–La Jaquita promenade, small black-sand beaches and natural pools. This provides a promising setting for routine coastal walks. The landscape is volcanic, with built promenades rather than pine-forest paths.

Dogs

Guía de Isora council explicitly identifies taking animals onto beaches and pools as sanctionable. Plan around permitted public walking routes, not unrestricted access to the sand or natural pools. Confirm current signs and leash rules for each route.

Restaurants / winter life

Muelle Viejo, at Pasaje el Ancla 1, is a concrete waterfront dining lead. Town life makes Alcalá worth investigating, but January–February opening patterns and dog-friendly terraces have not been confirmed.

House + garden reality

No house meeting garden, pets and an easy walk to both restaurants and sea was established. Rural properties advertised under the wider Guía de Isora name must not be confused with staying in Alcalá itself. A rooftop terrace is not a substitute for the garden requirement.

Why consider it: the daily-life picture is appealing: promenade, coves and local dinners. Why hesitate: finding the right house may be harder than finding the right town; the large-dog ferry condition remains.

02

Guía de Isora · Southwest Tenerife

Playa San Juan

First investigationRental terms unresolved

The strongest everyday-life candidate: harbour, beach, cafés and independent restaurants, with actual house leads to investigate.

The scene / British influence

Official tourism describes a beach used by visitors and locals throughout the year. Our judgment: a functional Canarian seaside town with international visitors, rather than a place whose identity is a luxury resort. It still is not a “no Brits” hideaway; no reliable nationality share was found.

Daytime warmth

21.7°C January / 22.0°C February at the regional Tenerife South station. The official beach guide specifically says the harbour and breakwater shelter the beach from waves and wind—a useful local feature, without promising all-day warmth.

Coast & everyday walking

A roughly 360-metre black-sand beach, accessible waterfront and a promenade with restaurants. It looks well suited to ordinary morning and evening outings. The bay and built coast have a different feel from Cap Ferret’s long ocean beaches and wooded peninsula.

Dogs

The same Guía de Isora beach/pool restrictions apply as in Alcalá. Being able to walk to the beach does not mean the dogs can walk on it. Establish usable promenade routes and garden suitability before selecting a rental.

Restaurants / winter life

La Boca Rie, Avenida Emigrante 30, is a French/Mediterranean dining example in the correct town. The year-round visitor-and-local setting is encouraging; specific winter hours and terrace pet consent remain to be checked.

House + garden reality

Villa Marejadilla advertises a private garden, sea 100 metres away and pets by arrangement. Its published 3 January–30 April 2027 rate is €290/night, or €8,700 for 30 nights, before any long-stay agreement. A cheaper negotiated rate is not assumed.

Another tangible lead: Villa Green House advertises a private backyard, pets allowed and a five-minute walk to beach/restaurants. It is a multilevel house with a rooftop pool: check whether the ground-level yard is genuinely suitable, confirm both dogs, and obtain a current winter quote. No complete property match is established yet.

03

Alajeró · Southern La Gomera

Playa Santiago

Quiet harbourLower priority

A calmer, smaller-scale possibility—but one whose housing, terrain and dining need to stand up to a long stay.

The scene / British influence

A harbour, promenade and terraces offer the appealing picture of unhurried coastal life. Island tourism also calls it one of La Gomera’s main tourism centres, so describing it as undiscovered or tourism-free would be misleading. British visitor influence was not quantified.

Daytime warmth

A local January/February normal was not independently verified. It would be misleading to print Tenerife South’s 22°C figure as Playa Santiago’s own climate. That evidence gap counts against it in a warmth-led decision.

Coast & everyday walking

The island guide describes about a kilometre of beach beside the fishing/sports harbour and a promenade. Beyond the coastal strip, the dry volcanic slopes make this a different walking proposition from flat wooded paths. Check gradients from any house, not just distance on a map.

Dogs

Specific permission for dogs on the main beach was not verified. The official tourism guidance warns that most beaches prohibit pets and directs visitors to check each location. Neither a quiet beach nor low season establishes permission.

Restaurants / winter life

Tasca Enyesque is a specific local dining lead. Our judgment: attractive for a quiet stay, but the smaller restaurant circuit needs checking carefully before committing for an extended period. Future opening schedules are unverified.

House + garden reality

No fully matching rental was found. A villa marketed at Pueblo Don Thomas above the golf course illustrates a recurring problem: private outdoor space can come with a hillside location rather than a simple walk to harbour restaurants. Add an inter-island journey to the mainland ferry question.

Why consider it: calm harbour life. Why it ranks lower: unverified local climate figures, a smaller dining circuit and no proven easy-walking house. Do not book a hillside villa on the strength of the town’s seafront photographs.

04

Mogán · Southwest Gran Canaria

Arguineguín

Everyday convenienceNordic-oriented services

A useful practical comparison: fishing-town roots, winter life and possible dog-beach access, with established Nordic-oriented services.

The scene / British influence

The official island guide describes a fishing atmosphere within a major tourism area. A local Scandinavian supermarket establishes Nordic-oriented commerce, not the size of a community. Our judgment: a different flavour from a British resort strip, but still an expat-influenced town—not a discreet Cap Ferret equivalent.

Daytime warmth

20.8°C January / 21.2°C February at Gran Canaria Airport. That station is on the east coast, so it is a regional reference, not proof of Arguineguín’s precise exposure or a comparison accurate to tenths of a degree.

Coast & everyday walking

Harbour life and seafront walks are the main attraction. It is a developed coastal town, rather than a pine-backed villa village. The appeal is practical daily living; whether it feels sufficiently understated is a personal fit judgment.

Dogs

February 2026 reports describe a designated pet area at El Perchel and timed access at La Lajilla (without a demarcated pet zone), with access 07:00–10:00 and 18:00–21:00. This is a useful lead, but the municipal rules and current signs must be confirmed: these reported arrangements are not a guarantee for winter 2027.

Restaurants / winter life

Cofradía de Pescadores, Avenida del Muelle 63, is a concrete harbour dining lead. The fishing-town and tourism setting makes it worth checking for everyday life; individual winter restaurant schedules remain unverified.

House + garden reality

No exact garden, pets and short-walk match was established. Be particularly careful with “La Playa de Arguineguín” listing labels: inland Salobre golf villas must not be counted as houses within walking distance of the town coast.

Why consider it: potentially convenient and a better dog-beach prospect. Why hesitate: Nordic-oriented services do not establish a low British visitor share or recreate Cap Ferret. Housing and the mainland ferry condition remain unresolved.

05

Agadir coast · Morocco

Taghazout

Surf-world cosmopolitanDifferent proposition

The strongest climate-supported alternative on the African mainland, but its appeal is international surf culture rather than Hamptons-style polish.

The scene / British influence

Morocco’s tourism board describes fishing-village origins, surfing, yoga, hostels, villas and luxury hotels. Our judgment: bohemian and international, with a younger surf energy. Do not confuse the village with the planned Taghazout Bay resort development. International surf tourism is not a promise of no British visitors.

Daytime warmth

21.1°C January / 22.0°C February at Agadir Inezganne, based on official 1991–2020 normals. Nights average 8.4 / 10.2°C, noticeably cooler than the Canary station references. The exposed coast can feel different from the station; no precise local wind-adjusted figure is claimed.

Coast & everyday walking

Atlantic sand, fishing boats and surf points give it a stronger beach landscape than the small Tenerife coves. The actual winter photograph makes the village’s scale and built setting visible. It does not offer Cap Ferret’s wooded walking environment.

Dogs

Local beach permission and everyday conditions around free-roaming dogs need checking rather than assuming an open beach is suitable. More fundamentally, entering Morocco and returning to the EU require a documented veterinary plan, including the rabies-titre requirement described below.

Restaurants / winter life

Windy Bay Restaurant is one specific village lead. The tourism offer supports a surf-and-café interpretation, not a verified Cap Ferret-level restaurant scene. Exact winter hours and pet acceptance are unverified.

House + garden reality

A researched Taghazout Bay villa advertises a private garden, but its rule permits pets only outside/in the garden. It was rejected as a normal house stay with dogs. No fully matching rental was established, and “minutes from the beach” does not prove walkability to village restaurants.

Why consider it: warm-afternoon climate and a distinct local/surf identity. Why hesitate: it changes the nature of the holiday, has cooler evenings, and the pet/house arrangements need considerably more work. Keep it as an alternative, not an automatic escape from the Canary ferry problem.

05 / The tempting alternatives

Why the first shortlist changed.

Marbella & its orbit

Excluded on the stated atmosphere preference. Nearby luxury residential areas are not a solution merely because they are quieter than the centre. The regional winter temperatures are also below the desired daytime range.

Sotogrande

The earlier recommendation over-weighted affluence. A spread-out international residential and marina setting is not sufficient evidence of the desired local village atmosphere, and no warmth advantage meeting the target was established.

Roche, Conil & Zahara

Worth remembering for another season. The Atlantic landscape is attractive, but the Cádiz reference averages only 16–17°C daytime highs. Their exact winter openings were not verified, so neither warmth nor restaurant life is assumed.

Madeira

Funchal’s verified highs are about 20°C in January and February. That makes it marginal for a firm 20–22°C preference; it does not prove the weather of Calheta or Ponta do Sol. A suitable mainland car-ferry route was not established.

Mainland Portugal

No mainland Portuguese candidate with evidenced 20–22°C winter afternoons was established in this screening. It is not advanced as a warmth-led recommendation. This is a screening limit, not a claim that every coastal town was exhaustively researched.

Essaouira & northern islands

Tempting atmosphere leads, but this research did not establish the required combination of daytime warmth and sheltered conditions. They are not ranked against the verified southern station data. A future comparison would need their own official local climate evidence.

06 / The practical test

The journey can decide the destination.

Canaries: the cabin limit matters.

Cars and pet cabins exist on mainland–Canary ferries. Fred. Olsen publishes about 33 hours to the first island and 40 hours to the second on its Huelva route, depending on sailing order. A checked September Armas Cádiz–Gran Canaria sailing showed 34h30m. These are not confirmed winter-2027 departures.

Both checked cabin policies describe a 30kg pet limit. Armas explicitly allows at most two pets, each no more than 30kg. Fred. Olsen’s Huelva policy describes two pets up to 30kg. That does not establish a suitable cabin arrangement for a heavier dog.

A written acceptance for the exact vessel and animals is needed before relying on an exception. Two cabins do not automatically bypass a per-animal limit. No exception, pet-cabin inventory or future fare was verified. La Gomera would also need an inter-island connection with its own pet arrangements.

Fred. Olsen route · Pet policy · Armas cabin policy · Armas route

Morocco: plan the return paperwork.

The EU’s official guidance, checked with Morocco selected, requires a successful rabies antibody titration test for entry from Morocco. It normally requires a three-month wait from blood sampling before EU entry. The precise timing and documentary route must be established using the animals’ records.

The EU’s stated re-entry waiver depends on the successful pre-departure test being recorded in an EU pet passport. Do not assume a UK animal health certificate automatically provides that waiver. Morocco’s ONSSA publishes country-specific entry certificates; the correct certificate and endorsement for a UK-origin journey via Spain still need veterinary confirmation.

For return to Great Britain via Europe, dogs also normally require vet-recorded tapeworm treatment 24–120 hours before entry. A suitable Spain–Morocco car-ferry pet arrangement has not been verified here. Morocco is not transport-ready simply because the ocean crossing is shorter in principle.

Practical conclusion: neither route is ready to book on the evidence collected. Keep the attractive destinations in view, but establish pet transport or veterinary feasibility before paying for a long rental.

A sensible order of decisions

Choose a workable place, then a house.

  1. Resolve the mainland–Canaries cabin question first. A published limit or a written exception must cover the actual dog, not merely say “pets welcome”.
  2. If the journey works, investigate Playa San Juan first and Alcalá second. Obtain a real winter monthly quote, exact location, photographs of the ground-level garden, secure boundaries and explicit two-dog consent. Verify the walk to restaurants and the sea.
  3. Use Arguineguín as the practical counterpoint. Its reported dog-beach provision is promising, but the expat scene may not be the atmosphere sought.
  4. Keep Taghazout only if the surf-village proposition appeals. Clear the veterinary and transport requirements before treating it as a workable alternative. Playa Santiago remains lower priority until local climate and walkable housing are better established.

No house, fare, exception or restaurant reservation has been booked. The clearest result is a focused direction for the next enquiry—not a claim that every part of the brief has already been solved.

How the judgments were made

Warmth, atmosphere and practical fit are considered separately. A town is not called suitable merely because its regional average maximum reaches 20°C. Winter dining, daily walking, housing type and transport all matter. Town impressions are qualitative interpretations of the cited destination descriptions and specific listings, not demographic statistics or first-hand claims. Missing information stays visible.
