Start here / Choosing the area

My recommendation:
Dénia first, Tavira for a second stay.

For a long January–February stay, I would choose Dénia as the strongest overall starting point. If trying two places, I would add Tavira. Neither recreates Cap Ferret: this pairing gives the best-supported combination of local town life, restaurants and coastal surroundings within the remaining shortlist.

1. Dénia — my first choice for winter

Why I choose it: the food market, fishing quarter, harbour and documented February restaurant activity offer a convincing everyday routine beyond the holiday season. That answers an important part of the Cap Ferret brief: being able to go out, eat well and enjoy the coast without the whole stay revolving around a resort.

Where to focus: Baix la Mar and the centre-to-Marineta Cassiana side for town and waterfront access; consider Les Rotes for the coastal setting, recognising that it is a different, more spread-out base.

The compromise: Dénia is a larger working port town, with more urban surroundings and less of Cap Ferret’s pine-and-sand village character. I choose it for winter living, not because it looks most like Cap Ferret. Alicante’s 17.0 / 17.6°C January/February highs are a distant regional reference, not verified Dénia temperatures.

2. Tavira — my choice for a contrasting stay

Why I choose it: its historic centre, riverfront and Portuguese restaurants offer a quieter town setting, with explicit winter dining evidence in Tavira and nearby Cabanas. For the understated side of your Cap Ferret preference, this is a stronger second comparison than another Costa Blanca town.

Where to focus: Tavira itself if restaurants and walking around a town matter most. Choose Cabanas only if you prefer a smaller lagoon-front base; it does not put Tavira’s restaurants on your doorstep. Fuseta is another smaller fishing-town comparison.

The compromise: Tavira is on a river, not directly on an ocean beach. Island beaches require separate access. Faro’s regional highs are 16.3 / 17.0°C, so this is an atmosphere choice rather than a warmth upgrade; the drive is also longer.

Temperature: an important limit

These choices offer regional average afternoon highs around 16–18°C, not dependable warm beach weather. No evidence here supports ranking the towns by fractions of a degree. If reliable 20°C-plus days are essential, this mainland shortlist does not meet that requirement. San José’s drier regional climate is worth considering, but it is not a proven temperature upgrade.

Atmosphere and dogs

I have favoured working town centres, local food markets and fishing districts over villa developments and resort strips. That addresses the atmosphere you want; no town can be promised free of British visitors, and nationality figures do not establish the street experience.

Coastal walking and permission to take dogs onto sand are separate. Dénia’s public waterfront and the Tavira/Cabanas public streets offer useful everyday walking settings; neither recommendation assumes unrestricted beach access. Conil’s dog restrictions materially reduce its appeal for this trip.

Two-stay option / choose the experience first

Which two locations make sense?

My preferred combination is Dénia + Tavira for variety and everyday winter life. Choose Dénia + San José instead if a drier coast and outdoor scenery matter more than a wider restaurant choice.

Dénia + Tavira

A Spanish working port and food-market town followed by a Portuguese historic river town. The contrast is in architecture, food and coastal landscape, rather than a demonstrated improvement in temperature.

This is my first choice for comparing two places you might enjoy returning to. Tavira is the town-life recommendation; Cabanas is a separate option if lagoon-front living appeals more than staying in the historic centre.

Dénia + San José

A larger town with a broad food scene followed by a smaller coastal village beside a rugged landscape. San José has credible year-round restaurant evidence, although the choice is narrower.

Almería’s lower regional rainfall is the reason to consider this alternative. It does not guarantee warmer afternoons, calm weather or unrestricted dog access to protected beaches. If an easy beachfront routine matters most, compare La Herradura as well.

There is no defensible temperature-based reason to assign one town to January and another to February: the station comparisons do not support that precision. Dates, transfer routes and accommodation can follow the area decision. If 20 hours from Coquelles is a strict maximum, the southern and Portuguese comparisons exceed it.

01 / Dénia: candidate for one stay

Costa Blanca

Dénia · Altea

17.0 / 17.6°CJanuary / February average highsAlicante city · 1981–2010

Town life first; the villa second

Dénia’s permanent food market, Baix la Mar fishing quarter and restaurant streets make the centre the strongest starting point for everyday winter life. Its council recorded 43 participating venues in the February–March 2026 tapas route: useful historical evidence of a functioning winter food scene, without promising the same programme in 2027.

Altea offers a different emphasis: a hilltop old town, galleries, the Palau arts centre and university arts activity. That is a credible cultural identity of its own. The old town, seafront and hillside villa developments are distinct settings; proximity to Benidorm does not make them interchangeable.

International, with a Spanish town centre

Both towns attract international residents and visitors. Local reporting puts UK nationals at about 2.8% of Dénia’s registered residents at the end of 2024; this says nothing reliable about winter visitor shares or a particular street. The case for Dénia rests on its market, neighbourhoods and dining evidence, rather than a promise about who will be there.

Where a garden house changes the experience

A house in outer Les Marines or on the Montgó slopes does not inherit the centre’s walkable restaurant choice. Start with a real entrance and walking route: distance, gradients and pavements matter. Les Rotes seafood restaurants can be attractive destinations while remaining too far from a rental for an everyday stroll.

On the lower Montgó slopes, El Campús, Camí Corral de Calafat 5, publishes dinner service, a terrace equipped for winter and ordinary hours until midnight, Tuesday closed. This is a useful local lead, not proof that an unspecified hillside villa is walkable. Altea Hills needs the same scrutiny.

Winter dining conclusion

Dénia has the stronger documented breadth of town life. Altea’s Bon Vent supplies unusually clear recurring winter hours, although its early Sunday–Thursday closing limits dinner choice. Neither town’s restaurant evidence settles the separate house-and-garden requirement.

02 / Bay and promenade

Costa Tropical

La Herradura · Almuñécar

16.8 / 17.7°CJanuary / February average highsMálaga Airport · 1981–2010

A bay-centred way of living

La Herradura’s appeal is concrete: a curved bay, seafront promenade and restaurants along Paseo Andrés Segovia. For the Cap Ferret brief, that compact relationship between home, water and meals is the attraction. Almuñécar is the larger neighbouring town; Marina del Este and Cantarriján are separate dining destinations, not extensions of the same easy beachfront walk.

Atmosphere: promising, with a location test

The bay setting supports an understated coastal stay, but that is an assessment of its layout rather than measured evidence about its clientele. International residents and visitors are part of the area. There is no sound basis here for promising a British-free setting or ranking its winter social life above Dénia’s.

What winter research actually established

The restaurant websites verify several distinct choices, but most do not publish clear January–February service patterns. La Barraca publishes daily hours and a Valentine’s page; the local chiringuito association describes off-season weekend paella at Chambao de Joaquín. These are useful winter clues, not five confirmed restaurants open every evening.

Choose the walking route carefully

The promenade and public streets offer accompanied dog walks; sand access is a separate question. A garden villa above the bay can turn a short map distance into a steep climb. Main-bay accommodation has a different daily routine from a house above Marina del Este or near an outlying cove.

03 / Historic town; lagoon coast

Eastern Algarve

Tavira · Cabanas · Fuseta · Cacela Velha

16.3 / 17.0°CJanuary / February average highsFaro · 1991–2020

Tavira for town life; Cabanas for the waterfront

Tavira’s historic streets, squares and riverfront offer the most convincing base here for a varied daily routine. The restaurant research found several central businesses publishing lunch and dinner hours, including an explicit winter timetable at Franganário. Cabanas provides a smaller lagoon-front setting with a separately verified winter restaurant; it is not a Tavira-centre neighbourhood.

Fuseta is another smaller lagoon-front option. Cacela Velha works better as a day-visit comparison in this research: its charm has not been matched by evidence of a similarly deep winter dining choice for a long stay.

Understated does not mean isolated

The old-town and lagoon settings are closer to the requested quiet coastal character than a large entertainment resort. That is a place-based assessment, not a claim about nationality. International visitors are part of the Algarve; reliable winter visitor shares for these individual towns were not established.

Better evidence of a winter routine

Franganário and Sabores da Ria explicitly distinguish winter opening from summer. Primitivo and Zeca da Bica supply ordinary weekly timetables. This gives Tavira/Cabanas a firmer practical dining case than relying on summer beach photographs or a restaurant’s existence alone.

The coast is a different arrangement

Tavira stands on a river; Cabanas and Fuseta face the lagoon. Mainland waterfront strolling is not the same as stepping onto an Atlantic beach. Island access, boat arrangements and permitted dog routes need checking independently. A private garden house should be tested against the chosen town’s actual restaurant cluster, not the wider region.

04 / Low regional rainfall

Cabo de Gata

Agua Amarga · San José

16.9 / 17.6°CJanuary / February average highsAlmería Airport · 1981–2010

San José for a stay; Agua Amarga for comparison

The landscape is volcanic, dry and open, with coves and whitewashed settlements: a different pleasure from Cap Ferret’s pines and Atlantic sands. Local tourism-business association ASEMPARNA describes San José as the park’s more urban, family-oriented settlement and Agua Amarga as the smaller, chic option. Those are destination descriptions, not visitor-nationality measurements.

A stronger winter dining finding

San José now has substantive support for a winter routine: the association’s business guide specifically classifies five named restaurants as open all year. Casa Miguel’s own off-season service pattern and Guía Repsol’s account of its continuing trade when seasonal businesses close reinforce that finding.

The brochure is historical/undated, hosted under a 2024 path. It establishes a recurring pattern rather than promising every dinner in 2027. The five names also vary in appeal; open-all-year status alone is not a quality award.

Village charm versus service depth

Agua Amarga remains appealing for its scale, but its winter dining evidence is thinner. La Chumbera publishes lunch/dinner hours, Wednesday closure and a November holiday; that is useful, without proving a January schedule. La Villa’s “rest of season” timetable does not explicitly include January–February. San José’s 4nudos publishes July hours, which cannot be carried into winter.

Walking and the house

San José’s town and port restaurants give a concrete cluster to test against an actual house entrance. Protected coast access and permitted dog routes remain separate from restaurant proximity. Casa Miguel advertises “Dog Friendly”; indoor seating and individual pet conditions still need confirmation.

A garden, a central map pin and a nearby beach do not by themselves prove a comfortable 15-minute walking route. Verify the actual entrance, gradients and public access before choosing the house.

05 / Atlantic beaches; access restrictions

Costa de la Luz

Conil · Roche · Zahara de los Atunes

16.0 / 16.8°CJanuary / February average highsCádiz · 1981–2010

Conil is the practical town base

For Atlantic beaches and a routine built around walking out to eat, investigate Conil town before the wider villa coast. Its published restaurant timetables provide several concrete lunch-and-dinner options. Roche’s pinewoods and garden villas may resemble the desired setting more closely, but a villa development does not supply Conil’s town-centre dining on foot.

Zahara de los Atunes remains a separate comparison with its own restaurant and property checks. Evidence from Conil cannot establish winter service in Zahara or a convenient daily routine in Roche.

Assess the street, not a nationality label

The attraction here is the Atlantic landscape and Spanish coastal-town layout. Reliable winter visitor-nationality shares were not established. Choose the immediate neighbourhood for its open businesses, walking routes and residential feel rather than assuming the wider coast has one uniform atmosphere.

More dinner evidence than the first pass suggested

El Mercado, Francisco Fontanilla, El Roqueo and La Almazara publish actual dinner hours. Their sites do not label these as January–February commitments, but they give a stronger shortlist than a generic warning about seasonal closure. Weekly closing days differ, which is useful when testing a realistic routine.

Dog rules can decide the location

Conil’s published rules allow animals on urban beaches outside May–October, while natural beaches are prohibited without authorisation. Its animal ordinance requires dogs over 20kg to be muzzled with a resistant non-extending lead. These rules cannot be carried across to Zahara. Identify the precise beach and permitted public route beside a prospective house.
